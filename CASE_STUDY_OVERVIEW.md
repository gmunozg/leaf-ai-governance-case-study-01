# Case Study Overview

## The business problem

AI adoption often moves faster than the internal processes needed to govern it.

A company may know that a team wants to use an AI tool, but still lack a clear answer to questions such as:

- What is the intended use?
- What types of information may be involved?
- Who should review the use case?
- What evidence is needed before a decision is made?
- What conditions should apply?
- How should the decision be documented and revisited?

## The approach

This project explored how those questions can be organized into a repeatable governance process.

Rather than treating AI governance as a static policy exercise, the project focused on decision-making, accountability, evidence and traceability.

The work was informed by the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and designed as a simulated enterprise implementation.

## What was achieved

The project produced a working governance prototype that demonstrates how an AI use case can move through structured review while preserving human oversight and documentation.

The prototype was tested in a synthetic QA environment to confirm that governance conditions could be enforced and recorded.

## What is intentionally not public

The public case study does not disclose:

- proprietary scoring or thresholds
- internal routing rules
- vendor assessment questions
- review matrices
- control libraries
- acceptance criteria
- automation logic
- database schemas
- reusable templates
- detailed QA logic

These materials are treated as internal LEAF methodology.
